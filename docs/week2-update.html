Does adding the glasses help?

Accuracy below is AUC: 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect. The wrist bar is real data. Every glasses bar is a simulation built from published research, so treat it as a plan, not a result.

Wrist alone (real people)
0.886
Glasses alone, one test (simulated)
0.76
Wrist + glasses, one test (simulated)
0.89

0.92
0.5 coin flip1.0 perfect

Plain reading: bars show the middle case, where the two sensors overlap a little. If they gave fully separate clues, glasses would add about +0.02 (one test) or +0.05 (all tests) over the wrist alone. At the middle case it is +0.01 and +0.04. If both mostly pick up the same severity, the one-test gain disappears. A pilot of 30 patients and 30 healthy people would show a gain in 94% of runs (separate clues), 78% (middle) or 57% (heavy overlap) with one test, and 98–100% with all tests.

Why this is optimistic: the simulation ignores camera noise, medication, age matching, and the fact that the research studies disagree with each other. "All tests" also assumes we first measure healthy volunteers on our own glasses.

Can it tell if the wearer has Parkinson's?

Not as a diagnosis. It can say how closely a wearer's numbers match published Parkinson's groups versus published healthy groups, then merge that with the wrist score. Published values it uses:

MeasureHealthyParkinson'sSource
Antisaccade error rate (looks at the dot instead of away)19%34%Waldthaler 2021, 39 studies
Antisaccade reaction time369 ms412 mssame paper, 34 studies
Pupil light reflex: speed, size, delay–slower, smaller, later2025 review, 9–10 studies
Ordinary look-at-the-dot reaction time–about 19 ms longerChambers 2010, 47 studies

The studies disagree a lot and used lab equipment. Reaction times and light-reflex numbers shift with our camera and screen, so they only count once healthy volunteers set our own baseline.

Words used here

Saccade
A fast eye jump to a new spot.
Antisaccade
Look the opposite way from a dot. Hard for the brain, and harder with Parkinson's.
Pupil light reflex
How the pupil shrinks when a light flashes.
AUC
One number for accuracy. 0.5 is chance, 1.0 is perfect.
Simulation
A computer-made test with known answers. Good for planning, not proof.

Research prototype. Nothing here is clinically validated and nothing here diagnoses Parkinson's disease.
