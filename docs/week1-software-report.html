Eye tracking: what a laptop webcam can and can't measure

We simulated eyes with known true values and measured them the way each camera would. A 30 fps webcam gets timing and accuracy roughly right but cannot see fast or tiny movements.

Pupil size cannot be measured at all with a normal webcam; it needs infrared light.

What we changed because of it

  • New protocol with three 5-second drift checks that recalibrate before each task. In simulation with the same drift, saccade gain went from 0.83 (wrong) to 0.95 (true value).
  • A live warning on screen when the head moves more than 4° from where it was at calibration.
  • Timed tasks use only horizontal eye position; the webcam's vertical signal is too noisy.

For the hardware team

The biggest error source on a laptop webcam is the head moving relative to the camera. A camera fixed to the head (the planned eye-tracking glasses) removes that problem. For pupil size and fast eye movements, aim for an infrared camera at 120 fps or more with 850 nm IR LEDs. Please have a mentor check the LED eye-safety design before any testing.

For the wrist and gait teams

Upload your results in the shared format in docs/DATA_FORMAT.md. Ask Srijan for a device key, add your measurement names to the registry before uploading, and log a sync_tap event so your timestamps line up with the eye data. The best open wrist dataset to start with is PADS (469 people, Apple Watch on both wrists, non-commercial license).
